# Elicit

An expert's advantage is mostly in what they attend to before starting. A model knows all of it but
works only from what the prompt says, and so fails in five ways: it solves the request as stated when
something else would serve better (wrong problem); it works from digested sources and familiar methods
and judges itself (shallow); it is correct and forgettable (plain); it states shaky things as fact
(overconfident); it adds more instead of better (bloated). "Act as an expert" fixes none of these. What
fixes them is the content of experts' attention written into the prompt, with reasons. This skill writes
it, from what you already know. Do no research. Do not carry the prompt out.

## Convene the panel

Name six to ten people (real masters, or roles where no name fits) whose combined judgement would make
this exceptional, and for each, what they would insist on and why. Include:

- the masters of every field the result depends on, especially fields the request does not name;
- for each quality the result should have as its audience feels it (break "cool" or "good" into those
  qualities), whoever in any medium is best at producing exactly that quality;
- someone who would question whether this is the right thing to make at all;
- someone who would try to break it, refute it or misuse it;
- a master of method, whose way of working (testing, estimating, subtracting) fits this task.

Name only people and works you are sure of. A name without what they would insist on adds nothing.

## Think it through

From the panel, settle:

- the real goal, who it is for, in what situation, and the effect it should have; if a different thing
  would serve the goal better, say so in one sentence and still write the brief for what was asked;
- the rawest material to start from, instead of summaries;
- the principles and methods, with their reasons, and the mistake people usually make here;
- what is uncertain or likely wrong, and what must be checked against a primary source;
- the best example of this kind anyone has made, and what to take from it;
- the two to four moments the audience will remember, described beat by beat in every sense the medium
  has; the line between delightful and tacky; one stretch idea a master wouldn't have thought of;
- what to leave out: cut scope, never craft.

## Write

First the panel: one line each, a name and what they bring. Then the brief, in a code block, in the
user's voice, as a senior practitioner would hand it to a capable colleague: full sentences, reasons,
the judgement calls made openly. Give delight and taste the most room; correctness is the floor. Carry
each panel member's principles with their reasons and credit. Length is fine. End with how to tell it
is done: checks someone other than the doer could make, including one for delight.
