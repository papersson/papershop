# Origins: a journey to a modern system

Use for explicitly history-led films. The modern system supplies the opening question and
the destination; history explains how it became possible, why its choices made sense, and
which assumptions or commitments remain. A chronology pasted beneath a lecture is insufficient.
Ordinary explainers keep their usual story workflow.

## The story is a route

Open with a concrete modern capability, constraint or surprising behavior. Travel to the
people, places, artifacts and immediate problems that explain it. Follow more than one
historical thread when their convergence matters. Return to the opening system with a
new explanation of its strengths and costs. Do not default to the oldest event as the opening.

Make the timeline a persistent navigable world. The camera can enter a room, machine,
document or experiment, follow an idea into another context, then pull back to reveal its
connections. Distinct settings and changes of scale make travel legible. Preserve landmarks
and threads across transitions so the viewer knows when, where and why they moved.
Dates and labels orient the viewer; the events and encounters carry the film.

For a history of programming practice, let the investigation carry the route. Reconstruct what
a programmer could edit, run, inspect and infer in a documented environment from each period.
Show the change to the program, the resulting evidence and the next decision. Historical
context should determine the system's boundaries, available tools, access and measurement
costs; a period label or vintage screen alone is insufficient. Dates locate these encounters,
not the invention of every technique. Practices overlap, and older methods remain useful.
Distinguish documented historical practice from an illustrative reconstruction executed on
modern hardware. Never make newly added instrumentation reveal evidence from an earlier run.

A mechanism explanation belongs inside an encounter: someone has a problem, an artifact
reveals a solution, the consequence changes what becomes possible next. Return to the route
after the explanation. Breaths follow discoveries and section endings; avoid constant camera
motion or an endless march of labeled cards. Use 3D only when spatial relationships teach
something; depth, parallax and travel do not by themselves require a 3D model.

## Causal history, with evidence

For each consequential link, record the dated event, contemporary constraint, technical choice,
later consequence and source. Classify edges as documented influence, enabling condition,
independent convergence or conceptual comparison. Visual resemblance is not evidence of ancestry.
Distinguish invention, publication, deployment and adoption when their dates differ.

Describe designs as responses to their context, not inevitable steps toward today's winner.
Show a relevant alternative or abandoned branch when it changes the explanation. Separate an
intrinsic limitation from a historical constraint and from the cost of changing an installed
ecosystem. A blank-slate design thought experiment is useful only when its assumptions and
migration costs are explicit. Do not turn the user's anecdote about weekend inventions into
a claim that all foundational systems were improvised or poorly designed.

Verify the modern endpoint against current primary sources. A historical predecessor such
as Erlang can explain a modern Elixir or Scala/Akka destination without becoming the subject
by default. Do not call one implementation universally state of the art.

## Reference packet and brief

When a personal reference library is available, retrieve only relevant whole-film notes and
passages. Preserve reference IDs, source time ranges, exact user notes and their scope. Distinguish
user selections from assistant suggestions, and user reactions from automated reviews. A liked
film is a development reference, not a factual oracle or approval of every scene. Keep held-out
evaluation notes out of production packets. Personal media stays in the user's library.

Inspect the actual selected reference, including transitions and audio, before claiming a
comparison. If access is limited to stills, a transcript or a description, say so. Extract the
technique (continuity, discovery, orientation, rhythm), then create original imagery and wording.

Write `research/brief.md` with:

- The modern opening question and the changed understanding at the return.
- A route of encounters: date/place, immediate problem, artifact/action, consequence and source.
- The connection map with edge types and any uncertainty.
- The world and camera plan: persistent landmarks, transitions, scale changes and rests.
- A small reference packet explaining what to learn from each selection, plus exclusions.
- The factual questions and visual/pacing risks that review must resolve.

The brief need not fill a fixed number of stops. Keep the film's length proportional to the
discoveries it asks the viewer to make. Existing story authorization still counts.

## Review the journey

Review moving transitions alongside still frames. Check whether a viewer can reconstruct the
route, explain the original constraint, identify a consequential connection, distinguish a
documented influence from a comparison, and use the history to reason about the modern system.
Check that closing imagery pays off the opening question. A pretty timeline or correct dates
alone do not establish understanding. Save reviewer suggestions separately from user reactions.
For histories of practice, also check whether the viewer can follow the programmer's actual
actions, explain why those tools fit that environment and choose the next useful measurement.

Reusable request:

> Make an origins film about [modern system/capability]. Start with [concrete modern question],
> then take us through the people, places, artifacts and constraints that explain how we got
> here. Let a persistent timeline and connected scenes carry the journey. Show the consequential
> convergence, the alternatives and the commitments that remain. Return to the modern question
> with a deeper explanation. Use [reference IDs/passages] for the named techniques, with original
> imagery. Verify causal links and today's endpoint; mark uncertain links explicitly.
