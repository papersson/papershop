# Audience level

`video.json.level` controls assumed knowledge, scaffolding and detail. Drive controls who can
judge subject correctness. Runtime, production effort and review needs are separate decisions.
Both levels use `pedagogy.md`, an approved learning brief and an independent student script pass
for teaching explainers. A short beginner video still needs a coherent chain.

## intro (default)

Usually 3–4 key ideas, taught through a small recurring example and explicit intermediate steps.
Explain intuition before formulas. Aim for two or three quantities to remember; real record values,
line numbers and identifiers are counted separately. A real example can recur throughout. Give
one useful scope statement rather than a catalogue of caveats. Use the kit's scene components.

A previous evidence-heavy search video became a “barrage of details.” The remedy is to subordinate
measurements to the model, not prohibit real data or skip review. Verify specific claims against
sources or runs; research unfamiliar material and the actual repo behind a repository explainer.
Choose runtime after the learning brief. The 20-minute first-cut and five-minute local-revision
budgets are advisory forecasts; use structural-round estimates for new chapters or changed arcs.

## deep-dive

More mechanisms, evidence, assumptions and edge cases for a viewer who needs them. Use recorded
runs, expert/student/editor script review, full-resolution sheets and independent frame review.
The default effort forecast is 60 minutes for the first cut and ten for a local revision;
structural revisions default to four times the local budget and can be overridden in video.json.
`thorough` allows more investigation and review rounds, not unnecessary breadth.
