Part D, hash-table feedback (2026-10-25): “the visuals just didn't feel very delightful at all,
and they didn't feel like the centerpiece”; “a standard lecture, but just with stunning visuals”;
“some pauses are just too long”. The user also named real typing and button clicks as good sound,
and reaffirmed the sphere opening, Blender hard drive and tunnel game-feel pass. Therefore put
finished style frames before the shoot, welcome visual richness and textbook breadth, restrict
sound to touch and size holds to new content. The earlier austerity and fixed holds over-corrected
crowding; preserve one thing at a time, full sentences, canonical level and no barrage of numbers.
Evidence: ~/drafts/studio-phase2/evidence/findings.md.

where we should use 3D.” Hence the hard 3D admission test.

Part F, hash-table feedback (2026-10-25): “I think it looks really nice. But maybe doesn't breath
enough, maybe too much visuals and stuff going on?”; “Maybe breath after each section and each
important point, but otherwise feel free to move around visuals and animations etc?” Replace
per-change holds with still, silent rests at named key points and longer section breaks with a
stage reset. Let related motion flow between them and leave unrelated things still. Breathing
depends on where rests fall, not how many tiny holds are inserted. Evidence:
~/drafts/studio-phase2/STEP-F.md.

Narration feedback (2026-10-27): “I think the primary weakness of the videos currently are
related to the narration”; “The script is not that great, I think it has to be much more engaging
and natural. Use full sentences, use "we", etc.” Write narration to [script.md](../script.md): one
person talking, a question kept open, the misconception voiced and broken, caveats in the Evidence

contractions. Evidence: the measurements and before/after pairs in script.md; worked example:
[script-example.md](../script-example.md). On 2026-10-27, after hearing the rewrite over the same

than the best YouTube explainers, which is expected for now)”.

Observability feedback (2026-10-27): “I do like them.” The user nevertheless felt the
observability film “focuses too much on \"events\" in history” and preferred “seeing what the
programmer would do at their computer to make things observable (and then observing).” They
clarified: “But the historical context is still important!! I want to understand how a programmer
would have solved this problem at different points in the last decades”. For this kind of
history, show period-specific investigations: edit, run, inspect evidence, reason and try again.
Keep historical constraints and context central. Preserve this as a history-of-practice option
within [origins.md](../origins.md), not a rule replacing every historical journey. Exact feedback
and scope: verdict `gen-how-we-got-here-observability-cut3`; positive
batch feedback also applies to gen-how-we-got-here-deployment-cut2.

Web prologue feedback (2026-11-02): “I want her to understand why these ideas were needed”;
“keep it focused on the problems rather than lots of historical details.” For these novice
origins films, let a concrete pain motivate each solution, keep the human stories that advance
that chain, and name the real concept plainly. The user challenged “But a connection needs more
than a cable” because no cable had been introduced. Therefore audit each sentence's premise and
referent against what the viewer already knows. They also asked to “pause the narration
appropiately so it doesn't disturb the animation/motion” during the LOGIN attempt: give a
performed sequence enough silence for its actions and consequences to register. “First film
has burned in captions, don't like it” reaffirms the separate-caption rule above. The audience
and high-level scope apply to this pair, not automatically to every Studio film. Evidence:
the user's chat notes quoted here; corresponding production briefs:
How a click reaches you (`films/web-0a-how-a-click-reaches-you/research/brief.md` in studio-journey)
and Where is the page made? (`films/web-0b-where-is-the-page-made/research/brief.md` in studio-journey).

TCP film sketches (2026-11-11), reactions to looping sketches of the second cut's hero scene; the
director had not yet watched the finished cut when this was written: “just be careful there is not
too much movements going on at once; take it slow, otherwise it risks looking and feeling
busy/overwhelming”; “Clearly and slowly and clearly show this update, make sure to get the viewers
attention. And of course while you have drawn the attention there, don't interrupt it by doing
other stuff in the lifeline diagram above it.” Afterwards, as a principle: “be super careful about
the viewers attention -- focus it on singular things at the screen at all times (or most time, or
well when singular and focused attention is important)”. Hence one place to look for a change the
viewer must read, and a consequence elsewhere as its own beat. This narrows Part F and keeps it:
motion still flows between key points, and a change nobody has to read is owed no hold. Shown one
at a time, the exchange then looked turn-based, twice: “Does it look like the client sends, then
waits for server, then sends again?”; “it now looks like the sender always sends a burst of its
data, and then the server responds.” Hence the replay at true pace, and the question the panel's
viewer and the attention reviewer now answer: what would a viewer wrongly conclude from this
order? “I think we should clearly signify how there is no ACK on segment 2 that was lost”: draw an
absence and give it a beat. Two notes on the first cut repeated rules already written (“I don't
want just boxes everywhere”; “Remove all references to "the real run"”), from a build that accepted
its own style frames; hence the written check of every "never" when the director has not seen the
frames. Evidence: the user's chat notes quoted here; the session's
[retrospective](https://claude.ai/artifact/78Abqd2JaUeKD37A261o9H). The film lives in a cloud
session's scratch folder (`tcp-video-v2`), not yet in studio-journey.
