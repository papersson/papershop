# An explainer played for laughs

Read this only when the user asks for comedy, cartoon acting or slapstick. A request for something
engaging still follows `../taste.md`. Comedy adds acting and sound while preserving the explanation.

Make each gag act out the idea the viewer has just heard. Write its meaning in the screen note,
so a joke about a cached answer visibly means a cached answer. Preserve the mechanism: a copy
leaves the original in place. A funny false implication is especially easy to remember.

Keep the diagram clean. Let characters and props carry the gag while component names and colours
keep their meanings. Plant the prop or expectation before the payoff. Give the anticipation a
pause, then land the hit on its cue. A two-part pattern can set up a third that breaks it; use
escalation when the repeated idea earns the time. A cut to the aftermath lets the viewer supply
an obvious missing action. Reactions and callbacks can clarify how to read the result.

Give poses a clear line of action and readable silhouettes. Let eyes lead a move and a blink
mark a thought. Reserve extreme takes for the strongest surprise. Keep each character's shape
language consistent, so its identity survives a change in pose or scale.

Build effects from the time-based motion helpers in `../kit.md`. Use seeded scatter, inked puffs,
impact stars or a brief smear when they help a physical action read. A high-contrast impact frame
belongs on a rare major hit. Keep camera shake short and away from labels being read. A shared
helper is a shared file for the main session to maintain.

Layer a physical hit with a transient and a body in the same acoustic room. Use the mix bus for
the shared tail. A small recurring sound can identify a character. Let loud effects decay in
narration pauses; a limiter cannot restore a word masked by a sound. Place every contact on the
same named chapter cue as the picture. Listen to the result and inspect its sound sheet.

Give a gag enough time for setup, pause and reaction in the brief and reel. More acting can make
the film longer. End with a calm picture where the mechanism and its outcome are clear.

The chapter reviewer considers whether the gag reads at normal speed, expresses the intended
mechanism and leaves the focal point on the explanation. Findings become suggestions on the
desk through the same single chapter pass. The user's response judges whether the gag works.
